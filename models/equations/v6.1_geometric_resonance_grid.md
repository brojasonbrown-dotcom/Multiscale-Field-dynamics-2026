## 7. Resonance → Dimensional Balance (frictionless, naturally sustained)  `[E]`

**Why resonance sustains.** A normal mode x_j(t) = A_j·e^{iωt} is an eigenstate of the quadratic
Hamiltonian. Three precise consequences:

1. **Frictionless.** In the ideal (undamped) lattice, each mode's energy is conserved:
   dE/dt = 0. No energy leaks out — the oscillation persists forever. Real systems add damping
   γ (true friction); a high-Q resonance is "near-frictionless." **Correction to the earlier
   ontology:** "incommensurability = friction" is a mislabel. Incommensurability is *conservative
   beating* — energy sloshes between modes but is never lost — while friction is *damping* —
   energy leaving into an environment. Different physics.

2. **Naturally sustained.** A mode is stationary up to a phase: it does not decay, it only
   accumulates e^{iωt}. This is *why* a resonance sustains itself — it is an eigenstate, not a
   driven transient.

3. **Dimensional balance.** The eigenvector's amplitude ratios A_j are the *balanced*
   configuration across coupled dimensions: each dimension carries a fixed fraction of the total
   energy with **zero net transfer** between them. In-phase = symmetric (balanced together);
   out-of-phase = antisymmetric (balanced in opposition). An *imbalance* is a superposition of
   two non-degenerate modes, which **beats** — energy periodically transfers back and forth.
   Balance = a single eigenmode; imbalance = a beating superposition.

**Formal statement.** For a quadratic H, the normal modes diagonalize it: H = Σ_k ω_k a_k†a_k.
Each mode is decoupled (no cross terms = frictionless) and conserved (eigenstate = naturally
sustained). "Dimensional balance" is literally **diagonalization**: the balanced states are the
eigenmodes; the ω² > 0 modes are the stable (sustained) ones, and the ω² < 0 modes are the
unstable (decaying) ones.

**What this makes rigorous.** "Stability and instability are both possible, and stability

structures are the ω² > 0 (and, for persistence against decay, gapped) eigenmodes; the unstable
